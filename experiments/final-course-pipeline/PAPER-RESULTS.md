# Technical results for the paper

Across all 520 moments, detailed self-choice passed 357 cases (68.7%), Jev-directed policy selection passed 379 (72.9%), and OpenAI Decisions-directed selection passed 382 (73.5%). Jev improved on self-choice by 4.2 percentage points, but the 95% student-cluster bootstrap interval crossed zero (-1.0 to 9.2). Decisions improved by 4.8 points (0.2 to 9.5). Neither router exceeded the fixed rigor policy, which passed 385 cases (74.0%). These are exploratory first-reply scores under our rubric, not a replication of the published multi-turn leaderboard.

The pattern changed within the pre-existing strict matched mathematics frame. Among its 346 cases, self-choice passed 249 (72.0%), Jev 252 (72.8%), Decisions 260 (75.1%), and fixed rigor 263 (76.0%). Jev's difference from self-choice was 0.9 points (-5.3 to 6.5); the Decisions difference was 3.2 points (-2.2 to 8.3). This frame also imposes conversational-cut, duplication and usable-response requirements; differences from the full preview cannot be attributed solely to subject matter.

Routing changed the balance of tutoring moves. Across the 260 rigor moments, Jev increased passing replies from 149 to 180, while passes on the 260 scaffolding moments fell from 208 to 199. In the matched math frame, the corresponding counts were 89 to 104 for rigor and 160 to 148 for scaffolding. A fixed rigor instruction performed well partly because it did not prohibit supportive moves: its replies were rated as containing both scaffolding and rigor in 202/520 cases. The policy name therefore does not establish the behavior actually produced.

Choosing the assigned category was also insufficient for a successful response. Of Jev's 376 category matches, 320 replies passed (85.1%); among 144 mismatches, 59 still passed (41.0%). Selecting the forced policy corresponding to the teacher-assigned category passed 438/520 (84.2%), or 299/346 (86.4%) in the matched math frame. This diagnostic exposes execution failures that remain even when the category is supplied. It is not a deployable policy or evidence of student learning.

Evaluator choice mattered. On the same fixed 32 cases, Gemini rated self-choice/Jev at 20/19 passes, whereas Claude rated them at 16/22. Across the 96 actual replies, pass/fail agreement was 78.1% (Cohen's kappa .551). This audit does not establish a robust Jev advantage. Generation used 1,560 valid replies with no truncation. The new experiment, including both evaluators and conservative interrupted-call reservations, cost $3.00. Median generator first-answer latency was 1.20 seconds for self-choice and 1.24 seconds for Jev-selected replies. Adding historical Jev inference gave an estimated serial median of 1.43 seconds; because the router was cached and calls ran concurrently, this does not demonstrate a latency improvement.

Overall, the contribution is a paired policy-selection analysis showing why category agreement, response quality and simple fixed-policy controls must be evaluated separately. These data support a useful negative result: the tested routers did not establish an advantage over a strong fixed instruction, particularly in the matched mathematics frame.
