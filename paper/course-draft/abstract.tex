AI tutors must support learners without completing the reasoning they need to practice. We examine this tension using TutorMoments-Preview. A qualitative analysis of 38 matched moments codes the mathematical work left by plain and evaluation-aware responses. In the first AI-assisted coding pass, evaluation-aware responses invited connections, explanations, or strategies in 16/38 cases, versus 2/38 plain responses. A separate experiment generates 1,560 first replies with Qwen3-32B across all 520 benchmark moments. Archived Jev and OpenAI Decisions outputs select between matched scaffold and rigor replies. Under automated evaluation, pass rates rose from 68.7\% for detailed self-choice to 72.9\% and 73.5\%, but fixed rigor guidance reached 74.0\%. Jev's gain shrank to 0.9 percentage points in the strict matched mathematics frame. The findings distinguish category agreement from response execution and show why routing claims require fixed-policy controls and inspection of the work left for learners.
