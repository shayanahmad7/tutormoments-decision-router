AI tutors must help students without completing the mathematical reasoning they need to practice. Using TutorMoments-Preview, a dataset of real tutoring conversations, we examine this problem in two ways. First, we code the work left for learners in 38 moments. Explicit instructions about balancing support and challenge produced more replies inviting connections, explanations, or strategies than general tutoring guidance. Second, we design an experiment in which a decision model chooses scaffolding or rigor and a language model writes the tutor's reply. Across 520 moments, we compare two decision systems, Jev and OpenAI Decisions, with the tutor choosing its own approach and with fixed instructions. Automated first-reply pass rates were 68.7\% for self-choice, 72.9\% with Jev, 73.5\% with Decisions, and 74.0\% with rigor instructions at every moment. Decision-guided replies had higher pass rates than self-choice, but neither system outperformed fixed rigor guidance.
